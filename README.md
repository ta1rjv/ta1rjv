## 🧭 About

> I am an **amateur radio operator** focused on **RF systems, software-defined radio, digital communications, signal processing, embedded systems and experimental communication technologies**.

My work sits at the boundary between **hardware and software** — from theory and mathematics to the actual system: how a signal is generated, processed, transmitted, propagated, received, measured and interpreted.

Rather than treating disciplines independently, I look at the **complete system and the interfaces between its components**. RF hardware, DSP, software, embedded platforms, antennas, propagation and measurement are often different parts of the same engineering problem.

## 🔬 How I Approach Engineering

I approach technical problems from the **system level first**. Before accepting a result, I try to understand the assumptions behind it, the physical mechanism responsible, and the limitations of the model or measurement.

```text
Problem  →  Physical Model  →  Analysis  →  Implementation
   ↑                                              ↓
Documentation  ←  Validation  ←  Measurement  ←──┘
```

The important part is the **feedback loop between theory and reality**:

- A **simulation** shows what *should* happen.
- A **datasheet** describes what a component is *expected* to do.
- A **measurement** shows what the system *actually* does.

Understanding the differences between those three is a significant part of engineering work.

That combination is where many of the most interesting problems exist.

## 🧱 Principles

- **Measurable** — rather than purely qualitative
- **Reproducible** — rather than dependent on undocumented assumptions
- **Physically consistent** — rather than purely empirical
- **Transparent about uncertainty** — rather than overly confident
- **Documented** — rather than dependent on personal knowledge

> The objective is not simply to make a system work — it is to understand **why it works, when it works, when it fails, and how the result can be reproduced**.
