## About

I am an **RF engineer focused on radio frequency systems, software-defined radio, digital communications, signal processing, embedded systems and experimental communication technologies**.

My work sits at the boundary between **hardware and software**. I am interested in how an idea moves from theory and mathematics into an actual system: how a signal is generated, processed, transmitted, propagated, received, measured and interpreted.

Rather than treating these disciplines independently, I prefer to look at the **complete system and the interfaces between its components**. RF hardware, DSP, software, embedded platforms, antennas, propagation and measurement are often different parts of the same engineering problem.

My interests extend from practical RF development and measurement to propagation research, digital communication systems, amateur radio and open-source engineering tools.

## How I Approach Engineering

I tend to approach technical problems from the **system level first**.

Before accepting a result, I try to understand the assumptions behind it, the physical mechanism responsible for the observed behaviour, and the limitations of the model or measurement.

A typical workflow looks like this:

```text
Problem
   ↓
Physical Model
   ↓
Analysis
   ↓
Implementation
   ↓
Measurement
   ↓
Validation
   ↓
Documentation
```

The important part is the feedback loop between **theory and reality**.

A simulation can show what should happen.

A datasheet can describe what a component is expected to do.

A measurement shows what the implemented system actually does.

Understanding the differences between those three is a significant part of engineering work.

## Research & Development

A recurring theme in my work is turning technically incomplete information into something that can be **understood, tested and reproduced**.

This includes investigating undocumented systems, reconstructing the behaviour of unfamiliar hardware, comparing theoretical expectations with measured data, developing experimental software, and building tools that make complex technical information easier to analyse.

I am particularly interested in problems where the answer cannot simply be obtained by searching for a single datasheet or copying an existing implementation.

In those cases, engineering becomes a combination of:

**documentation research + physical reasoning + experimentation + measurement + software**

That combination is where many of the most interesting problems exist.

## Principles

I generally prefer engineering work that is:

* **Measurable** rather than purely qualitative
* **Reproducible** rather than dependent on undocumented assumptions
* **Physically consistent** rather than purely empirical
* **Transparent about uncertainty** rather than overly confident
* **Documented** rather than dependent on personal knowledge

The objective is not simply to make a system work.

The objective is to understand **why it works, when it works, when it fails, and how the result can be reproduced**.
